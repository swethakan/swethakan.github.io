Adding

FLASHCARDS

to FranklinCovey’s learning platform

Context

Franklincovey sells leadership courses to companies to complete online or in-person. Physical courses are much more popular due to the materials learners receive like flashcards. In this feature we moved these physical flashcard onto our digital platform

My Role

I was the sole product designer and researcher. I worked with my product manager to decide how this feature will function and appear.

Year

2024

See Prototype

The Journey

Discover

We used interviews, competitive analysis, and more to understand what parts of the physical card experience needed to be replicated digitally to make this feature usable

Define

We understood the needs of learners and class facilitators and set out to create an easily navigable card deck that combined 2 decks of cards for our most popular course

Ideate

We configured the cards in different layouts, tested our favorite flows with more users, and landed on a finished feature for mobile and desktop

Deliver

This feature was made for the release of our mobile app. It was released as part of a single course for v1, and, despite having a limited release, increased mobile adoption by 5%

Goal: Combine 2 card decks for our upcoming course that are easily reviewable

After we defined our user needs we narrowed down the scope of the project both for business needs and to get the first version out the door in time for the launch of our mobile app. This feature was going to be a core part of the mobile platform and was intended to be a major draw for users to go ahead and download the app.

IDEATION

Exploring navigation, visual feedback, etc.

Challenges

Easy navigation

The cards came in 2 separate decks that had interrelated concepts. Users should be able to navigate between decks and concepts easily

Responsive design

The cards needed to primarily look great on mobile but also needed to be accessible on desktop since that was where a majority of learners were

Accessibility

The cards needed to be easily accessible outside of courses and during them. They also needed to have features that allow users to come back to specific concepts

Navigating between 2 decks of cards

The first digital cards was going to accompany the re-release of our most popular course, The 7 Habits of Highly Effective Leaders. This course had 2 card decks; one that reviewed the concepts and one that provided practice

Each deck had cards that included a front and back. This doubled the amount of information needed to show on the digital platform

We choose to position the practice cards close by the concepts they reviewed since users needed to be able to access these cards alongside their concepts. They had no need for 2 individual decks

The most obvious choice that leaders at the company supported was a linear layout for the cards which users could navigate by swiping left or right

Instead, we decided to combine both the decks together and lay them out in what we termed a “2D carousel” which users could navigate by swiping up and down for practicing conceptsOR right and left for reviewing all concepts together.

User testing the prototype

I tested the prototype with about 7 Clients, 4 learners, and a few internal folks. the response was positive with learners mentioning the convenience of these digital cards as a huge plus. Overwhelmingly, everyone I talked to preferred the ‘2D carousel’ layout of these cards.
